01 · The Market

What is commodity trading?

Commodities are interchangeable physical goods — energy, metals, agriculture — traded on organized exchanges via futures and options, or over-the-counter via swaps and forwards. Unlike equities, commodities cannot be valued with discounted cash flows: their price is set by physical supply, demand, inventories, seasonality and logistics. That makes them a uniquely quantitative playground where data, modeling and market microstructure matter more than corporate fundamentals.

⚡Energy

WTI & Brent crude, heating oil, gasoline cracks, natural gas (Henry Hub, TTF, JKM), power, coal. The most volatile complex — weather, geopolitics and storage constraints drive outsized moves.

🥇Metals

Precious (gold, silver) and industrial/base (copper, aluminum, zinc, nickel) plus emerging battery metals (lithium, cobalt). LME and COMEX dominate; contango/backwardation structures are heavily modeled.

🌾Agriculture & Softs

Grains (wheat, corn, soybeans), oilseeds, sugar, coffee, cocoa, cotton, live & feeder cattle. CBOT/CME is the hub; weather models, crop condition data and crush/spread relationships drive quant strategies.

Why quants fit here: commodity prices mean-revert within seasons, trend across cycles, and react to observable physical data (EIA inventories, USDA crops, weather). That creates statistically testable edges — carry, term-structure, spread and volatility trading — which is exactly the toolkit of a quantitative trader.
02 · Speak The Language

Industry terminology decoder

Filter by category. Every desk interview assumes you know these cold.

Futures contract Structure

A standardized agreement to buy/sell a commodity at a set price on a future date, traded on an exchange. Terms include the underlying, contract size (e.g., 1,000 bbl WTI), tick size, delivery month and settlement method.

Front month / deferred months Structure

The nearest expiring contract ("front") and later-dated ones ("deferreds"). Quants "roll" positions before expiry to maintain exposure; roll timing and liquidity shifts are tradable effects in themselves.

Open interest Structure

Total outstanding contracts not yet offset. Rising open interest with rising prices confirms trend strength; falling OI signals position unwind. A core feature in CTA signal construction.

Volume vs. liquidity Structure

Volume is daily trades; liquidity is how much you can trade without moving price. Thin deferred months force quants to model market impact explicitly when building multi-month portfolios.

Cleared vs. OTC Structure

Exchange-traded futures/options are cleared through a CCP (near-zero counterparty risk); swaps and forwards are OTC, increasingly cleared or collateralized post-Dodd-Frank/EMIR. Each leg has different funding and credit costs.

Contango Pricing

Forward curve upward-sloping: deferred prices above spot/front. Signals oversupply and high inventories. Long holders lose on the "roll yield" — a negative carry trap for naive index buyers.

Backwardation Pricing

Curve downward-sloping: spot above deferred. Signals tightness; longs earn positive roll yield ("convenience yield"). The classic scarcity signature in oil, gas and ags.

Roll yield / collateral yield Pricing

The return earned (or lost) by rolling a futures position through the curve. Total commodity index return = spot change + roll yield + collateral yield. Decomposing it is bread-and-butter quant analysis.

Basis Pricing

Local physical price minus the reference futures price (e.g., Cushing WTI vs NYMEX CL, Brazilian coffee vs KC). Basis trading monetizes location and quality differences with relatively low directional risk.

Crack spread Pricing

Refinery margin: value of products (gasoline, heating oil) minus cost of crude. Traded as crack spreads (e.g., 3-2-1). A mean-reverting relative-value staple of energy quant desks.

Spark spread Pricing

Power plant margin: electricity price minus fuel (gas/coal) cost adjusted for heat rate. The core P&L driver — and core hedging unit — for utility and commodity power traders.

Corn crush / soybean crush Pricing

Processing margin from crushing soybeans into meal and oil (analogous for corn into ethanol/DDGS). Crush spreads are liquid, modelable pairs on CBOT.

EFP / EFS Pricing

Exchange-for-Physical / Exchange-for-Swap: mechanisms to convert between futures positions and physical or swap positions at a negotiated basis. Bridges the paper and physical worlds.

Convergence Pricing

The tendency of futures price to meet spot at expiry (cost of carry pulls them together). Failure of convergence usually flags a delivery-point squeeze or logistics bottleneck.

Initial & variation margin Risk

Initial margin is the performance bond posted per contract; variation margin is daily mark-to-market cash flow. Quants must model margin spikes — vol regimes can double requirements overnight and force deleveraging.

Maintenance margin & margin call Risk

The floor below which equity must not fall; breach triggers a call to top back up to initial margin. Forced liquidations at this boundary create momentum cascades — a known microstructure effect.

VaR / CVaR Risk

Value-at-Risk: worst expected loss at a confidence level over a horizon. CVaR/ES measures tail severity beyond VaR. Commodity VaR needs fat-tailed, jump-diffusion treatment because prices gap on inventory prints and geopolitical shocks.

Greeks (delta, gamma, vega, theta) Risk

Sensitivities of option value to underlying, vol and time. Commodity options add "dollar Greeks" nuance because underlying is a storable good with its own curve dynamics.

Stop-loss & kill switches Risk

Pre-defined loss thresholds that automatically flatten a book. Systematic desks enforce hard max-drawdown limits and portfolio-level de-grossing rules.

Counterparty credit risk Risk

Risk an OTC counterparty defaults. Managed via CSA netting agreements, collateral, credit lines and XVA charges. Physically oriented firms (Traders & refiners) run large private books of bilateral credit.

Squeeze / corner Risk

When a dominant long forces shorts to cover at any price near expiry (e.g., nickel/LME 2022, oil derivatives). Position-limit rules and delivery-network analysis exist precisely to bound this risk.

Deliverable grade & warrants Physical

Exact quality specs a contract accepts for delivery; warehouse receipts ("warrants") prove stored metal/ag is deliverable. Deliverability drives quality premiums and inter-month spreads.

Delivery point & freight Physical

Where a contract physically settles (Cushing for WTI, Rotterdam/ARA for fuels). Freight differentials between hubs create tradable arbitrage (e.g., Brent-Dubai EFS, TTF–Henry Hub spreads).

Storage & carrying costs Physical

Rent, insurance, financing and spoilage. Full-cost-of-carry links spot and futures: F = S·e^{(r+u−y)T}. Storage capacity itself is priced — full tanks collapse contango; empty ones widen it.

Inventories & stocks-to-use Physical

Reported holdings (EIA weekly crude/gas, LME warehouse stocks, USDA stocks-to-use ratios). Low stocks amplify price sensitivity to supply shocks — a leading indicator engineered into many quant signals.

Seasonality Physical

Recurring calendar patterns: gas builds in summer/winters off, harvest pressure on grain, hurricane season in the Gulf. Seasonal decomposition is one of the oldest systematic commodity edges.

Hedging (long/short) Physical

Producers short futures to lock sale prices; consumers go long to cap input costs. Hedgers supply the risk transfer that speculators/quants are paid to absorb.

Arbitrage Physical

Riskless-ish profit from price inconsistencies: calendar spreads, location arb (netback), quality arb, cash-and-carry. Competition compresses arb windows to milliseconds — hence the quant arms race.

CTA / managed futures Quant

Commodity Trading Advisors run systematic trend/momentum programs across futures markets. Time-series and cross-sectional momentum remain the largest documented commodity factor.

Term-structure / carry factor Quant

Going long steeply backwardated markets and short contango ones harvest roll yield. One of the few factors that works specifically because commodities are physically stored, not cash-flowed.

Mean reversion Quant

Prices/spreads pulling back to fair value (crack spreads, inter-month spreads, after inventory shocks). Half-life estimation and regime detection decide when reversion is tradeable vs. structural break.

Alt-data Quant

Satellite imagery (tank shadows, crop NDVI), AIS ship tracking, pipeline flow telemetry, weather ensembles. Modern commodity quants compete on data pipelines as much as models.

Backtesting & overfitting Quant

Simulating strategies on history. Commodity backtests must handle contract rolls, changing margin, survivorship of listings and non-stationarity — otherwise results are fantasy.

Volatility trading / vol-meat Quant

Trading implied vs realized vol, dispersion and skew rather than direction. Energy vol books (gas, power) are among the most technically demanding seats on any desk.

03 · The Toolkit

Key skills required

Commodity quants sit at the intersection of mathematics, software and physical-market intuition. Roughly what hiring managers weight:

Probability & statistics (time series, Bayesian updating)Core
Stochastic calculus & derivatives pricingCore
Machine learning (gradient boosting, deep nets, NLP on news/weather)High
Optimization & portfolio constructionHigh
Python (pandas, numpy, statsmodels) — research & productionMust-have
C++ / Rust — execution & low-latency systemsStrong plus
SQL + data engineering (tick data, curves, inventories)Must-have
Domain sense: curves, storage, logistics, regulationDifferentiator

🧮Mathematics

Linear algebra, stochastic processes (GBM, OU jumps — the workhorse models for mean-reverting commodity prices), Monte Carlo, statistical arbitrage theory, cointegration/Kalman filters for spreads.

💻Engineering

Backtesting frameworks, event-driven architectures, kdb+/ClickHouse for tick storage, cloud pipelines (AWS/GCP), CI for trading code, and enough networking to reason about latency.

🗣️Communication & grit

Explaining a model's edge to PMs and risk managers, writing crisp trade rationales, staying disciplined through drawdowns. Desks hire people they trust under pressure.

Certifications that help (but don't substitute for a track record): CFA (general investing literacy), FRM (risk), professional weather trader (PWT) for energy/ag niches, and above all — a reproducible research portfolio: your own backtested commodity strategy with honest transaction costs.

05 · How Money Is Made

Quant strategy families on a commodity desk

📈Trend / momentum (CTA)

Time-series and cross-sectional momentum across 100+ futures. Slow decay, crisis-alpha profile. Entry seat for most systematic quants.

🔄Term-structure & carry

Long backwardation, short contango; harvest roll yield; model convenience yield and storage optimization.

↔️Statistical arbitrage / spreads

Crack, crush, spark spreads; inter-commodity (gas vs coal burn); pairs like gold/silver; cointegration and OU-based execution.

🌪️Event & data-alpha

Positioning around EIA/USDA releases, weather models, refinery outage feeds; NLP on news; satellite inventory nowcasting.

🎭Volatility & optionality

Implied-vs-realized vol, skew trades, straddle roll-downs, structured deals for corporates (vol meat desks).

🏗️Structuring / physical optimization

Real-options valuation of storage, shipping arbitrage, tolling agreements — quant methods applied directly to physical assets.

06 · The Ladder

Career path in commodity quant finance

Typical progression from campus hire to owning P&L. Timelines vary; skill gates don't.

Quantitative Researcher / Analyst (0–3 yrs)

Hedge funds · prop shops · bank quant teams

Signal research, data pipeline building, backtesting infrastructure, supporting senior PMs. You learn curves, contract specs and the graveyard of failed ideas. Degree: MFin/Math/Stats/Physics/CS helpful, not always mandatory.

Quantitative Developer / Risk Analyst (parallel entry)

Execution systems · market risk · margins

Production trading systems, curve calibration libraries, VaR/XVA, regulatory reporting (FRTB). Deep exit into either research or trading once you understand how books really move.

Senior Researcher / Portfolio Manager (4–8 yrs)

Own capital allocation

You run a sleeve: energy complex, grains, or cross-commodity factor portfolio. Accountable for Sharpe, drawdown, capacity and cost. Hiring shifts from credentials to demonstrated live track record.

Commodity Trader (execution → discretionary/systematic hybrid)

Banks · Vitol/Trafigura/Glencore · utilities

Physically-oriented firms offer a distinct route: junior trader → product specialist (crude, gas, metals) → head of trading. Quant skills supercharge traditional roles — basis, freight and optimization desks now recruit programmers.

Desk Head / Partner / Fund Founder (10+ yrs)

Own firm or lead a franchise

Launch a CTA/man-aged-futures fund, join as partner at a multi-strat, or lead a physical trading house's quant function. Your edge becomes platform + talent + data moats, not just models.

💰Compensation snapshot

Junior QR/QD: ~$120–250k total (prop/fund). Mid-level PM/trader: $300–800k with P&L bonuses scaling hard. Senior fund PM/partner: seven figures driven by performance fees. Physical trading houses pay similarly with larger upside on origination desks. Numbers vary by geography and firm economics.

🎯Who hires

Millennium/Citadel/XTX/Two Sigma/Winton (systematic); Goldman/Morgan Stanley/Citi (bank desks); Vitol, Trafigura, Glencore, Koch, Mercuria (physical); utilities & refiners (in-house trading); CFTC/exchanges for market-structure roles.

🧭Breaking in

Competitions ( Citadel Datathon, CME curriculum), research internships, a public backtest notebook, arXiv/papers on term structure, and fluency in one commodity complex end-to-end (wellhead to tank to futures).
